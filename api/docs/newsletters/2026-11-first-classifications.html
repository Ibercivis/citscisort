We have just published a first look at what the CitSciSort classifications are telling us: Method or object of study? What the first 1,700 CitSciSort classifications say.

So far, 60 people have made 1,698 classifications and given a first reading to 1,654 of the 12,855 abstracts (12.9%). A few things already stand out:

  • 46% of classifications see citizen science as the object of study, 37% as the method, and 18% are not sure.
  • In the natural sciences it is mostly a tool (entomology, zoology, marine biology, astronomy). In education it is mostly what is being studied: 31 of 39 classifications (79%).
  • The field seems to be maturing: among articles published in 2023, those that use citizen science as a method (46%) outnumber those that study it (38%) for the first time.
  • iNaturalist is by far the most mentioned platform (96 mentions), followed by Zooniverse (32) and eBird (28).

These results are provisional. Each abstract has only one reading so far, and the design asks for five different people per abstract, so every classification you add makes the picture more reliable.
