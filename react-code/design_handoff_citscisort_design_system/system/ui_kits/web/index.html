ABSTRACT · #4127 2 / 28 today

Urban bird diversity and citizen-collected occurrence records: patterns, biases, and opportunities

Authors
M. Haklay, A. Mazumdar, J. Wardlaw, P. Callaghan, L. Khan
Source
Global Ecology and Conservation · 2024 · doi:10.1016/j.gecco.2024.e02891
Keywords
citizen science biodiversity urban ecology sampling bias eBird participatory monitoring
Abstract
Urban environments support a surprising diversity of bird species, yet systematic monitoring in cities remains patchy. We analysed 1.2 million occurrence records contributed to citizen-science platforms across 38 European cities between 2018 and 2023 to examine species-richness patterns, spatial sampling biases, and the representativeness of recorded diversity relative to professional surveys.

Our results show that citizen-collected data recover 81% of species reported in concurrent professional atlases, but with substantial spatial bias toward green-space perimeters and residential areas with higher median income. We propose three low-effort platform changes — a weekly "gap square" prompt, an anonymised income-balanced sampling reward, and inline training on under-reported taxa — that in a pilot study reduced spatial bias by 34%. We discuss implications for data-quality frameworks and for urban-planning applications that increasingly rely on volunteered biodiversity data.
